# Case 5

**Give the pipeline:** an example chapter, plus notes for only two cards (Characters, for Ilse only, and Setup). Style, World, Entanglements, and the other characters have no notes and must come from the chapter.

## Example chapter

On the morning of the vote, Ilse Brandt climbed the council stairs the slow way, because her right leg had never liked them and because she wanted the extra minute.

Matthias Kroll was waiting on the landing with a leather folder held against his chest like a shield. He was twenty-six and still believed that a good briefing could prevent a bad day. "Councillor. We have a problem."

"We have had a problem since February."

"We have a new one." He lowered his voice. Two members of the northern bloc passed them on the stairs, nodded, and did not stop. "Hollander has gone over. He told me last night. He'll vote for the tariff."

Ilse stopped on the step. The grain tariff had been written to protect the Corn Exchange and the big estates, and it would put the price of rye out of reach in the river districts by midwinter. She had spent six weeks counting votes against it. The count had stood at twenty-one to nineteen in her favor, and she had been careful not to enjoy it.

"Then it's twenty to twenty."

"And a tie goes to the Speaker's casting vote."

"Which will go where the Speaker's casting votes always go."

"Which nobody has ever managed to predict," Matthias said carefully, "or prove."

They both knew what was whispered in the corridors: that the Speaker never broke a tie by conscience, that Aurel Voss had been breaking them the same way for eleven years, and that the way was decided long before anyone raised a hand. Nobody could say who paid. Nobody had ever been willing to ask the man who would know.

"You're meant to read me the other thing," Ilse said. "I can see it in the folder."

Matthias drew out a thin envelope, sealed with a green wafer. "From the Millers' League. Delivered to the office at seven. They want you to know they'll stand with you on the amendment, if you move it today."

"The amendment that would hand them a rebate."

"It would also hold the price of rye in your districts. They say both can be true."

She turned the envelope over. The League's seal, a wheel inside a circle, had been pressed hard enough to tear the paper. She opened it with her thumbnail, read the three lines, and handed it back. "They want it on the floor before the vote, not after. They want my name on it."

"Yes."

"And if it fails, my name is on a bill that took their side."

"Yes."

"And if I don't move it, the tariff passes and rye is out of reach by midwinter." She started up the stairs again, and he fell in beside her. "Matthias, what do you think I should do?"

"I think," he said, after a moment, "you've been rehearsing the speech for six weeks. I've heard you through the wall. It's very good. I think you should give it."

She smiled, in spite of everything, because it was true. She had rehearsed it in the bath and in the carriage and once, embarrassingly, in front of the butcher. She had never delivered a word of it aloud to any of the people it was written for.

At the top of the stairs the great doors stood open and the chamber hummed. Members drifted to their benches. Aurel Voss was already in the Speaker's chair, a silver-haired, mild-faced man who made a point of greeting everyone by name. His eyes met hers across the chamber and he inclined his head, pleasantly, as one does to a neighbor.

"Matthias," Ilse said quietly. "The envelope. Does it say who else knows about Hollander?"

"No."

"Then the League found out before I did. Which means someone talked to Hollander before the League did." She tucked a grey braid back under its pin. "Find out who. Quietly. And find out whether anyone has asked the Speaker to take lunch with them this week."

"That isn't proof."

"I'm not asking for proof. I'm asking what is going to happen at four o'clock." She took her seat, and the bell rang, and for the first time in six weeks she had no speech in her hands at all, only the envelope, warm from her pocket, and a number she didn't like.

## Card notes

### Characters: Ilse

Ilse Brandt, 44, woman. Councillor for the river districts. Heavyset, steel-grey braids pinned up, a limp in the right leg from a childhood fever. Wants to be remembered as honest, and also wants to win just once. Afraid of being the swing vote and getting blamed for it. Flaw: rehearses arguments forever and never says them. Values keeping promises. When cornered she gets formal and cold; when relaxed she tells terrible jokes. Speaks in full sentences, never swears.

### Setup

Ilse is the lead. Normal: she's been a backbencher for years, the one who reads every bill and nobody listens to. Disruption: a late defection leaves the tariff vote tied, and suddenly she matters. Six or seven chapters.
