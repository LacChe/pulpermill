## Chapter 2 Proposals

Here are three proposals for Chapter 2, each built around one of the combinations outlined above. All aim to take Pim Tallowby’s investigation of the stone one step further.

**Combination 1: Orla Fennick & Pim Tallowby (master/apprentice) + Weather Oven applewood rule**

**Option 1:** Pim wants to sneak away during the midday rush to sketch the stone alongside illustrations in Old Man Tiber’s herbals, hoping to find a match for its spiral pattern. Orla Fennick needs a perfectly fired batch of loaves for a special order from the Applewood Allotters, relying on Pim to carefully select the best applewood and keep the oven at a consistent heat. The clash happens when Pim, distracted by her sketching, uses a slightly moldy bundle, resulting in a smoky fire and a flat loaf. She stands to lose Orla's trust and the opportunity to continue working closely with her; the cost is being relegated to simpler tasks like sweeping floors. Orla, exasperated, grabs the stone from Pim’s hand, declaring it “a distraction for a girl who can’t even manage applewood,” but ultimately uses the smoky flavour of the loaf to impress the Allotters, attributing it to ‘depth of flavour’. The chapter ends with Pim feeling both useful and belittled.

**Option 2:** Pim wants to examine the Hearthstone closely while Orla fires the Weather Oven, hoping its warmth will reveal something about the stone's origin. Orla Fennick wants a perfectly predictable bake – meaning no fiddling around the Hearthstone, which she considers her domain. The clash occurs when Pim lingers too long, touching the Hearthstone as Orla’s hand hovers over it, and inadvertently shifts a small pile of ash revealing a hidden crack in the stone itself. Pim risks being seen as clumsy and disrespectful; losing this could mean a lessening of responsibility for firing the oven entirely. Orla concedes that the crack might be old, but declares it adds ‘character’, continuing to bake without interruption, but giving Pim one sharp glance when she gets too close again.

**Option 3:** Pim aims to compare her stone to stones collected by Corbin Reeds on his travels, hoping his cartographic eye will spot a regional origin. Orla Fennick needs Pim to keep the bakery floor swept and free of apple cores during a busy morning, a task often neglected when Pim gets distracted. The clash happens when Pim spills a basket of apple cores while rushing to show Corbin the stone, forcing Orla to stop kneading dough and clear the mess herself. Losing Orla’s patience means being put on cleaning duty for the rest of the week; Pim fears this will delay her investigation indefinitely. Orla, however, uses the spilled cores to create a quick apple sauce for customers, praising Pim’s unintentional ‘seasonal offering’.



**Combination 2: Corbin Reeds & Pim Tallowby (friendly acquaintance) + Loaf-sharing rule**

**Option 1:** Pim wants to share the stone with Corbin over tea and biscuits during his regular visit, hoping he'll offer a fresh perspective on its origin. Corbin Reeds, wanting to enjoy a quiet moment with Pim, expects her undivided attention. The clash occurs when a newcomer, Old Man Hemlock – notoriously stingy – arrives and requires a loaf. Pim feels pressured to present the *best* loaf, diverting her time from the stone and irritating Corbin. Losing Corbin’s company means missing out on potentially valuable insights; the cost is a deepening sense of loneliness in her investigation. In the end, the newcomer grudgingly accepts the loaf, and Corbin, understanding Pim's obligation, smiles and says he’ll wait, sketching Hemlock while they talk about the stone.

**Option 2:** Pim wants to use a loaf, freshly baked, as an offering to the newcomer in order to get his story about any stones he might have seen, using it as a pretext to examine him closely. Corbin Reeds wants Pim to join him on a quick sketch of the Millpond – a subject he’s been wanting to capture for weeks. The clash arises when the newcomer, a gruff woodcutter, barely acknowledges the loaf, preferring a hunk of cheese and asking curtly if they have applewood for sale. Losing the woodcutter's attention means missing a potential clue; the cost is a slowing down of her search. Corbin observes the interaction with amusement, sketching the woodcutter’s disdainful expression, and suggests Pim try bartering for information instead of offering bread.

**Option 3:** Pim aims to convince Corbin to trade one of his detailed sketches for a loaf specifically baked with applewood from the same bundle as the stone was found inside, believing the shared element may unlock something. Corbin Reeds wants Pim’s full attention while he explains his sketching technique, feeling she doesn't fully appreciate his art. The clash happens when another customer interrupts their conversation, requesting a specific type of loaf, forcing Pim to focus on baking instead of bartering. Losing Corbin’s trade means delaying her investigation; the cost is a frustrating sense of being stuck. He playfully accuses her of valuing stones over artistry, but ultimately trades the sketch for the loaf anyway, charmed by her enthusiasm.
