You are planning one chapter of a novel. A chapter is a scene, not a summary: someone wants something, something stands in the way, they have to choose, and by the end something is different. A writer will write the chapter from your plan, so make the plan a scene the writer can play, with people who act from who they are.

The tone
The genre and feeling of this story: Harbour noir, small-town and damp. Let it shape mood, conventions and texture, while events follow from the characters and situation. The narrator's habit of attention: She notices what people do with their hands when they lie. Meaning emerges from what the narrator notices and how it is weighed, and themes stay implicit in the telling. Let this decide what the narrator notices, in every scene. Humor is dry: understated, deadpan, found in precise observation. The narrator is wry: amused, observant, with a sideways view of events. Balance action and reflection, slowing for what matters and moving on quickly otherwise.

The world
The setting is called The Salt Coast. Era and technology in The Salt Coast: Late steam age: coal barges, telegraph lines to the capital, oil lamps on the quay, no telephones. Everyday life in The Salt Coast: Everything arrives by water and is taxed twice. Rent is paid weekly in coin, and a missed week is remembered for years.

Places in The Salt Coast, each shaping the people in it:
The Docks: Wet planks, coal dust and shouting. Crates stacked three high along the quay. What it does to people there: Everyone is watched, and everyone pretends not to watch. Used in 2 earlier chapters.
The Shop: A narrow harbour shop with a stove, a counter and a back door to the yard. What it does to people there: It makes people talk, because it is warm. Used in 3 earlier chapters.
The Board House: A grey stone building at the head of the quay, with one lit window at all hours. What it does to people there: People lower their voices as they pass. Used in 1 earlier chapter.

Things that matter in The Salt Coast:
The ledger: A fat black ledger with a cracked spine and a ribbon marker. Why it matters: It records who really paid for the dock repairs, and who did not. Held by Pekka. Used in 2 earlier chapters.
The brass key: A small brass key on a leather cord. Why it matters: It opens the cellar under the shop. Held by Suvi. Not used in any chapter yet.

Groups with power in The Salt Coast:
The Harbour Board: Seven men who govern the waterfront. It holds power by Licensing every boat, crate and cellar. Ordinary people feel it as a fee, a stamp and a visit on the first of the month.
The Dockhands' Brotherhood: A loose union of dock workers. It holds power by Slowing the work down when it is crossed. Ordinary people feel it as a hush in the tavern when a board man walks in.

What is true in The Salt Coast:
Rent is due on the first of the week (common knowledge). Cost: A missed week is a debt the landlord can call in at any time. When broken: It is punished: the landlord may take the goods.
The dock repairs were paid for by the shopkeepers, not the board (known only to a few). Cost: Saying so out loud invites a visit from the board. When broken: It is punished: licences are pulled.
The cellar under the shop floods on the spring tide (widely believed but false; the truth is: the cellar was sealed to hide the board's old accounts and has never flooded). Cost: Anything stored there is lost. When broken: It is shameful: the story is kept alive.
